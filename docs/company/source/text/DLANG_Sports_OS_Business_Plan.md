**01 EXECUTIVE SUMMARY**

**The business in one paragraph**

DLANG Sports OS is a Swiss-built operating system for the organisations
that run youth and club sport --- academies, camps, clubs, and the
schools and federations behind them. It replaces the six disconnected
tools a typical organisation runs on today (spreadsheet, WhatsApp, paper
consent forms, a bank account, a website, and the club president\'s
memory) with a single system that handles registration, payment,
scheduling, attendance, communication and reporting, in four languages,
on Swiss payment rails. It is built by an operator who runs a
145-athlete academy and needs the product himself.

**The ask, and a recommendation about it**

The deck asks for EUR 50,000 for 10% equity, at a EUR 450,000 pre-money
valuation --- CHF 418,500 at the model exchange rate. Two observations,
both in the founder\'s favour.

-   **The valuation is low, not high.** Swiss pre-seed pre-money
    typically runs CHF 0.5m--2.0m; European pre-seed sits around EUR
    2--4m. At CHF 418,500 pre-money, the founder is selling 10% below
    the market band, and setting a low reference price that the next
    round has to climb from.

-   **The instrument is wrong for the size.** A priced equity round in
    Switzerland requires notarisation twice, commercial register filing,
    and typically investor counsel at the company\'s cost. On CHF 46,500
    those fixed costs consume a double-digit percentage of the raise.
    Achieve identical economics with a subordinated convertible loan
    capped at CHF 418,500 pre-money --- equivalently CHF 465,000
    post-money.

**2.1 Fix the pricing contradiction this week**

Two prices are live in customer-facing assets. The investor deck prices
at EUR 149 per club per month plus EUR 2 per active athlete --- EUR 449
for a 150-athlete club. The pricing strategy document prices at CHF 290
/ 590 / 1,290 flat tiers. A prospect who sees both loses trust. A
founding club that signs one and is invoiced the other churns and tells
other clubs why.

The reconciliation is in section 5.3 and in the Pricing sheet of the
model: one formula --- base platform fee plus a per-active-athlete fee
--- presented as three packaged tiers. The tiers are the marketing; the
formula is the truth. It preserves the CHF 290 already promised to
founding clubs and it reproduces the deck\'s economics exactly on the
EUR track.

**2.3 Decide the canton, this month**

FIT Digital is the only Swiss funding instrument written for a
pre-revenue digital business-model startup at beta stage. Its Grant tier
is CHF 20,000, non-repayable, and it accepts companies pre-incorporation
or within six months of commercial-register entry. It requires
incorporation in Canton de Vaud and at least one team member domiciled
there. Geneva\'s equivalent (FONGIT) skews technology and life sciences,
and the cantonal business fund (FAE) serves the commercialisation phase,
not pre-revenue.

The next application deadline is 10 November 2026, with the selection
committee on 1 December 2026. Applications route through Innovaud, so
the pre-evaluation interview needs booking now. This is a dated,
high-value, low-effort action and it is the reason canton choice cannot
drift.

**2.6 Cap the founding-club discount and defend it**

The Strikefirst programme offers CHF 290/month locked for the lifetime
of the account. Against the revised public price of CHF 490 for the Club
tier (section 5.3), that is CHF 200 a month forgone --- CHF 24,000 per
club over ten years, or CHF 120,000 across five clubs. Two and a half
times the round being raised, given away before a line of the beta has
shipped.

That is a defensible price to pay for five reference customers,
design-partner input and the right to say \"we sold before we shipped.\"
It is not defensible for six, or eight, or \"one more because they were
nearly ready.\" Cap it at five, in writing, and use the cap as the
closing mechanism it was designed to be.

One correction to the offer itself: the founding table lists Starter at
CHF 290 for both founding and public customers, meaning a
sub-100-athlete founding club receives no price advantage at all ---
only a waived setup fee. Meanwhile the sales pitch markets \"CHF 290
versus CHF 590\" as the saving. Under the revised tier structure in
section 5.3 this resolves, because a 100--300 athlete academy --- the
stated primary ICP --- sits on the Club tier at CHF 490, so the founding
lock is a real and explainable CHF 200 a month.

**3.2 Why this founder**

Most software for club sport is built by software people who interviewed
club people. DLANG is built by someone who runs the organisation the
software is for: a 145+ athlete academy in the Lake Geneva region, with
camps, recruitment programmes, coaching staff, parent communications in
three languages and payments to chase. \[founder\]

That produces three advantages that are hard to buy.

-   **A live proving ground.** Every feature can be used in anger before
    it is sold. The first customer already exists, has real athletes and
    real money moving, and will tell the truth about what does not work.

-   **Buyer credibility.** A club president talking to another club
    president is a different conversation from a salesperson talking to
    a club president. In a market where distribution runs through
    personal networks, this is the primary channel, not a nice-to-have.

-   **Correct instincts about sequencing.** The internal documents get
    the priority order right --- money-moving workflows first, coach
    touchline second, everything else third. That ordering is not
    obvious from outside the operation and most competitors get it
    wrong.

The corresponding risk is equally real, and an investor will ask about
it: the founder is the product manager, the developer, the salesperson,
the support desk and the operator of the reference customer. Section 9
addresses this directly. It is the single largest execution risk in the
plan.

**4.5 The three real threats**

1.  **Spond.** Free at scale, already localised to CHF, already in
    Switzerland, and giving away its only paid product for twelve months
    here. It destroys willingness to pay for scheduling, messaging and
    rostering across the entire market. Every DLANG conversation will
    start with \"why not just use Spond, it\'s free?\" The answer must
    be rehearsed, numerical, and about Swiss payment rails.

2.  **Federation bundling.** Fairgate has cooperation agreements with
    Swiss-Ski and the Schweizerischer Turnverband under which member

    clubcorner.ch. Distribution in Swiss sport is captured at federation
    level, not club by club. Either target segments without a federation
    deal --- private academies, camps, international schools,
    multi-sport programmes --- or make a federation partnership the
    primary go-to-market. Trying to out-sell an incumbency club by club
    is the one strategy guaranteed to fail.

3.  **The CHF 250--300 per-year price floor, and Paak specifically.**

    has an AI assistant on the roadmap. It is building approximately

The fee is not margin --- founder-led onboarding is eight to ten hours

filter. A club unwilling to pay CHF 750 to set up will not pay CHF 490 a
month for two years, and a club that pays for onboarding turns up to the

**Payments: the deliberate decision to under-charge**

The model assumes 50% of a club\'s collections flow through the platform
on TWINT or card, at a net DLANG margin of 0.9%, with QR-bill free. At
140 athletes that is about CHF 24 per club per month --- roughly 5% of
revenue. Competitors take 2.5% to 3.9%.

This is intentional. Payment margin is the easiest revenue to add and
the fastest way to lose a Swiss treasurer. Under-charging on the rail
while charging properly for the platform is what makes the \"we are
cheaper than Spond on total cost\" argument true, and that argument is
worth far more than the margin foregone. Revisit at 100+ clubs, not
before.

**The uncomfortable number in this section**

The Pricing sheet of the model works the value case through on
deliberately conservative inputs: 20 admin hours recovered a month at
CHF 35 an hour, plus 45% of the CHF 7,560 of fees a typical club writes
off in a year. That totals about CHF 983 of value a month against a CHF
490 Club-tier price --- a ratio of roughly two to one, not the three to
one that the sales playbook requires before a deal is qualified.

That is not an error in the model. It is the finding, and it deserves to
be stated rather than buried. On generic assumptions a mid-sized Swiss
club barely clears twice the price. The ratio only reaches three times
where the organisation is larger, collects more, or writes off more ---
which is exactly the profile described in section 4.2 and section 7.1.
Two consequences follow, and both are already in this plan: never
qualify a deal on this generic model, only on the club\'s own audited
numbers; and if a club\'s own numbers come in below three times, walk
away rather than discount.

**5.4 The moat question, answered honestly**

An investor will ask what stops Paak, ClubDesk or Spond from doing this.
The honest answer has three parts, and none of them is technology.

1.  **Switching cost, once installed.** A club that runs registration,
    payment history, consent records and two seasons of attendance in
    one system does not move. This moat does not exist on day one; it is
    manufactured by getting the club fully migrated in the first 30
    days. That is why onboarding quality is a strategic function, not a
    support function.

2.  **Segment focus.** Generalist Verein tools will not rebuild around
    camp and programme commerce, because it is architecturally invasive
    and their existing base does not ask for it. That gives a two-year
    window, not a permanent moat --- but two years is enough to reach a
    few hundred clubs.

3.  **Distribution through the operator network.** The founder is inside
    the network he is selling to. That is not defensible in theory and
    it is extremely effective in practice for the first fifty customers.
    After that it must be replaced by referral mechanics and, ideally, a
    federation relationship.

What is not a moat: the AI suite, the design system, four-language
support, or \"being Swiss.\" Any of these can be matched within a year
by a funded competitor. Say so, and point at the three above instead. An
investor trusts a founder who can name what is not defensible.
