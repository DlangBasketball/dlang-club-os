1.4 What you would actually be building
This is not a CRUD app with a sports skin. The genuinely interesting problems, in the order they arrive:
  • Multi-tenant isolation that has to be provably correct. Every table carries a club identifier and row-level
    security is forced at the database. Children's data, medical notes and consent records. A leak is not a bug, it is
    the end of the company and a personal liability under the revised Federal Act on Data Protection.

  • Payment reconciliation on Swiss rails. TWINT, card and QR-bill, generated to Implementation Guidelines v2.4,
    with webhook handling, duplicate protection, refunds, and a reconciliation state that never leaves a parent
    believing their money has vanished. This is the hardest and most important thing in the product.
  • Offline-first attendance capture. A coach in a sports hall with no signal, wearing gloves, taking attendance in

  • Four-language everything, including outbound message translation, with tone preserved. Not a config flag —
    a first-pixel architectural decision.
  • A role-scoped AI layer, from Cycle 3. Per-club retrieval over payments, schedules and communications, with
    anonymisation before anything leaves the platform, human approval on every outbound message, and
    immediate escalation on anything touching health or child welfare.

    preparing the definitive agreements must calibrate duration, geography, scope and any required

8.2 Tax — the point most founders get wrong


     GRANT EARLY, AT NOMINAL VALUE, OR IT COSTS YOU REAL MONEY
     In Switzerland, receiving equity for services is generally a taxable benefit measured as the difference
     between what you pay for it and what it is worth at the moment of grant. Today the company is worth
     close to nothing, so a grant at nominal value creates close to no taxable benefit.
     Grant the same 20% eighteen months from now, with paying clubs and a closed round, and the same
     equity carries a taxable value — payable in cash, by you, in a year when your cash income from this is CHF
     48,000.
     The action: grant at incorporation, and consider asking the cantonal tax administration for an advance
     ruling to fix the valuation. That ruling is, after the conversion-price clause in the investor loan, the second
     most valuable piece of professional advice available to this company.



Two related points. First, if you would be working through your own company rather than as an individual, the
analysis changes and needs checking — including whether the arrangement risks being treated as disguised
employment, which is a live issue in Switzerland. Second, the services income is ordinary income and should be
invoiced properly, with VAT once either of us crosses the CHF 100,000 threshold.
